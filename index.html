Possessive
adjectives & pronouns

Un repaso claro para distinguir formas como her / hers, my / mine, our / ours y usarlas correctamente en frases cotidianas y de Mining English.

my / mine your / yours his her / hers our / ours their / theirs
One key idea:
Adjective + noun
Pronoun = no noun

1 · Understand the difference

Both forms show possession. The difference is what comes after them.

her

Possessive adjective

her + noun

Use her before the thing she owns.

Her helmet
Her gloves
Her radio
hers

Possessive pronoun

hers = no noun after it

Use hers when the noun is already understood.

The helmet is hers.
The gloves are hers.
That radio is hers.
Correct
This is her helmet.
This helmet is hers.
Incorrect
This is hers helmet. ✗
This helmet is her. ✗

2 · Apply the same rule

theytheirtheirsTheir tools. / The tools are theirs.
Fast rule

If there is a noun immediately after the possessive form, use the possessive adjective. If the noun disappears because we already know what thing we are talking about, use the possessive pronoun.

4 · Mining English Review

With the noun

This is my helmet.
That is her radio.
These are his gloves.
This is our locker.

Without the noun

This helmet is mine.
That radio is hers.
These gloves are his.
The locker is ours.
Useful question

Whose helmet is this? → It’s mine.
Whose gloves are these? → They’re his.
Is this radio yours? → Yes, it is.
